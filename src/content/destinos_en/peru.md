## Quick summary: why travel to Peru

Peru is not one destination: it is several countries in one. On the desert coast of Lima, you eat the best ceviche in the world. A 90-minute flight away, Cusco welcomes you with Inca streets and mountain air. An hour north, the Amazon of Iquitos immerses you in the most biodiverse jungle on the planet.

It is the ideal destination for travelers seeking cultural, archaeological, and natural variety in a single country. For those who want to combine colonial cities, pre-Columbian ruins, food experiences, and extreme nature without changing countries.

## When is the best time to visit Peru

### High, shoulder, and low season

The **high season** (July-August, Easter, Christmas and New Year) concentrates the largest tourist flow. Prices rise up to 30 % and Machu Picchu tickets sell out weeks in advance. It is the best time if you are looking for dry weather in the highlands.

The **shoulder season** (May-June and September-October) offers good weather in the highlands with fewer tourists. It is the ideal time to visit Cusco, the Sacred Valley, and Machu Picchu without crowds.

The **low season** (November-April, except holidays) brings rain in the highlands and jungle, but is the best time for the north coast (Máncora, Trujillo) and Lima. Prices drop significantly.

### Weather by region and packing recommendations

There is no single climate. In Lima, humidity is high and the sun appears from December to April (average temperature 72 °F / 22 °C). In Cusco, the climate is dry with warm days (68 °F / 20 °C) and nights that drop to 32 °F (0 °C) between May and August. In Iquitos, heat and humidity are constant (86 °F / 30 °C average, rain year-round).

Practical recommendation: travel in layers. A lightweight waterproof jacket, hiking shoes, sunscreen, hat, and breathable clothing will serve you in all regions. For the highlands, include a light thermal layer.

## What to see and do in Peru

### Must-sees by region

- **Lima**: Historic Center (Plaza Mayor, Cathedral), Miraflores and Barranco, Larco Museum, food tours
- **Cusco**: Plaza de Armas, Qoricancha, Sacsayhuamán, San Blas, San Pedro Market
- **Machu Picchu**: Inca citadel (circuits 1, 2, 3, or 4), Huayna Picchu, Machu Picchu Mountain
- **Arequipa**: White sillar historic center, Santa Catalina Monastery, volcanic viewpoints
- **Sacred Valley**: Pisac, Ollantaytambo, Moray, Maras, Chinchero
- **Lake Titicaca**: Uros floating islands, Taquile Island, Amantaní Island
- **Amazon**: Iquitos, Puerto Maldonado, Manu, nature reserves and lodges

### Unique excursions and experiences

Scenic flight over the Nazca Lines, sailing on Lake Titicaca, trekking the Colca Canyon (the deepest in the world), condor spotting, rafting on the Urubamba River, sandboarding in Huacachina, and birdwatching in the Amazon.

## How many days you need in Peru

### Quick stop (7 days)
Arrive in Lima (1 day), fly to Cusco the next day (3 days for acclimatization, Sacred Valley and Machu Picchu), return via Lima (1 day).

### Classic trip (10-12 days)
Lima (2 days), Cusco + Sacred Valley (4 days), Machu Picchu (1 day), Arequipa and Colca (3 days), return flight from Arequipa or Lima.

### Extended itinerary (14-21 days)
Add Puno and Lake Titicaca (3 days), the Amazon in Iquitos or Puerto Maldonado (3-4 days), the north coast with Trujillo and Chiclayo (3 days), or the southern route with Nazca, Paracas, and Huacachina (3 days).

## Estimated budget for traveling to Peru

### Sample budget per person per day

**Budget (30-50 USD/day)**: Hostels or guesthouses, market menus, public buses, shared tours, basic entry fees.

**Standard (70-120 USD/day)**: 3-star hotels, mid-range restaurants, economy domestic flights, group tours with guide, Expedition train to Machu Picchu.

**Premium (from 200 USD/day)**: 5-star hotels or lodges, fine dining restaurants, private tours with bilingual guide, Vistadome or Hiram Bingham train, flexible flight schedules.

## Altitude, safety, and health tips

### Altitude sickness: symptoms and how to prevent it

Cusco is at 11,155 ft (3,400 meters) above sea level. Altitude sickness (soroche) symptoms include headache, nausea, dizziness, and fatigue. To prevent them: arrive in Cusco the first day without strenuous activity, drink coca tea, stay hydrated, and avoid heavy meals. Most people adapt within 24-48 hours.

### Safe areas and common scams

Tourist areas of Lima (Miraflores, Barranco, San Isidro), Cusco (historic center), Arequipa (center), and the Sacred Valley are safe for travelers. The most common scams are unauthorized taxis (use Uber or ride-hailing apps), street currency exchange (use official exchange houses), and people offering tours without a license (only hire with verified agencies).

## Best tours and experiences in Peru

From Cusco you can take tours to the **Sacred Valley** (full day), **Machu Picchu** (full day or two days with option to climb Huayna Picchu), and the **Inca Trail** (4-day trek). From Lima, **food city tours** and excursions to **Paracas, Huacachina, and Nazca** are the most popular. From Arequipa, the **Colca Canyon** (2 days) is unmissable.
