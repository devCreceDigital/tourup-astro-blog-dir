## Quick summary: why visit Machu Picchu

Machu Picchu is not just a set of Inca ruins: it is a masterpiece of engineering, architecture, and harmony with nature. Built on a mountain summit at 7,972 ft (2,430 m), its perfectly fitted stone walls, agricultural terraces, and cloud-veiled location make it an experience that no other archaeological destination can match.

It is the ideal destination for travelers interested in pre-Columbian history, archaeology, landscape photography, and trekking experiences. It is also the main reason most international travelers include Peru on their destination list.

## When is the best time to visit Machu Picchu

### High, shoulder, and low season

The **high season** (May to September) offers clear days, ideal for photography and panoramic views. July and August are the peak months, with the daily visitor limit (currently 4,044 people per day) frequently reached.

The **shoulder season** (April and October) offers good weather with fewer visitors. September is one of the best months for its combination of stable weather and fewer visitors.

The **low season** (November to March) has frequent rain, especially in the afternoon. Advantages include greener landscapes, fewer visitors, and greater ticket availability, although rain can obscure the views.

### Monthly weather and packing recommendations

Machu Picchu has a subtropical climate. Temperatures range between 54-72 °F (12-22 °C) year-round. Humidity is high and rain is frequent from November to March.

Pack light clothing but always bring a rain jacket or poncho. Hiking shoes with good grip (the terrain is uneven and can be slippery). Sunscreen, insect repellent, hat, sunglasses, and a headlamp if you plan to arrive early. Documents: original passport (mandatory for entry) and the printed or digital ticket.

## What to see and do in Machu Picchu

### Must-sees inside the citadel

The **Main Plaza** and ceremonial sector. The **Temple of the Sun** or Torreón, with a window that aligns with the June solstice. The **Intihuatana**, the ritual Inca stone that functions as a solar clock. The **Temple of the 3 Windows** and the **Main Temple**. The **Industrial Sector** with storehouses and cultivation terraces. The **Guardhouse** and the **Sun Gate (Inti Punku)**, the most iconic spot for the sunrise photo.

### Complementary experiences

Climb **Huayna Picchu** (the mountain in classic photos, requires additional ticket and good physical condition). Climb **Machu Picchu Mountain** (panoramic view from higher altitude, less steep). Visit the **Inca Bridge (Q'eswachaka)**, an Inca trail carved into the rock. Take the **1-day Inca Trail** (short route from Km 104 of the railway). Visit **Aguas Calientes** (Machu Picchu Pueblo) with its hot springs and artisan market.

## How many days you need in Machu Picchu

### Express visit (1 day)

From Cusco, take the earliest train to Aguas Calientes (5:00-6:00 am), board the first bus to the citadel, 2-3 hour guided visit, free time for photos, and return. It is a long day but feasible.

### Classic visit (1.5-2 days)

Arrive in Aguas Calientes the previous afternoon. The next day, visit Machu Picchu early. If you have a ticket for Huayna Picchu or Machu Picchu Mountain, schedule the climb for the second shift (10:00-11:00 am). You can add a visit to the Aguas Calientes hot springs in the afternoon.

### Extended itinerary (3-4 days)

Combine Machu Picchu with the **Inca Trail** (4 days/3 nights), the **Salkantay route** (5 days), or the **Inca Jungle route** (3 days). You can also explore the Sacred Valley before or after your visit to the citadel.

## Estimated budget for visiting Machu Picchu

### Sample budget per person

**Budget (80-120 USD)**: general ticket + Expedition train + round-trip bus + lunch in Aguas Calientes.

**Standard (150-250 USD)**: general ticket + Vistadome train + bus + private guide in the citadel + restaurant meal.

**Premium (from 300 USD)**: ticket with Huayna Picchu or Machu Picchu Mountain + Hiram Bingham train (with breakfast and dinner onboard) + private guide + exclusive tour.

## Altitude, safety, and health tips

### Altitude and physical preparation

Machu Picchu is at 7,972 ft (2,430 m), significantly lower than Cusco (11,155 ft / 3,400 m). Most people do not feel altitude sickness here, but the physical effort of walking on Inca stairs (steep and uneven) is considerable. Wear shoes with good grip and bring enough water.

### Visit rules and recommendations

Large backpacks are not allowed (maximum 40x35x20 cm / 16x14x8 in). Trekking poles without rubber tips on the ends are prohibited. Drones, selfie sticks, professional filming equipment without permission, and eating inside the citadel are not allowed. Restrooms are outside the archaeological zone (in Aguas Calientes or at the ticket office). Bring your original passport: it is mandatory to present it at entry.

## Best tours and experiences in Machu Picchu

The most popular way to visit Machu Picchu is through a **full day from Cusco** that includes hotel-to-train-station transfer, Expedition or Vistadome train, bus to the citadel, professional guide, and return. For trekking lovers, the **4-day Inca Trail** is the most authentic experience: you walk on original Inca roads until reaching the Sun Gate at dawn. The **Salkantay route** (5 days) offers mountain and cloud forest landscapes without the Inca Trail's capacity restrictions.
