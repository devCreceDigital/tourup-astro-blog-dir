## Quick summary: why travel to Cusco

Cusco is not just a city: it is the living heart of the Inca Empire. Its cobblestone streets, colonial squares, and archaeological ruins coexist with artisan markets, contemporary Andean cuisine restaurants, and a cultural life that attracts travelers from around the world.

It is the ideal destination for lovers of history, archaeology, and Andean culture. All routes to Machu Picchu, the Sacred Valley, and the Inca Trail depart from Cusco, making it the most important tourism hub in Peru for any type of traveler.

## When is the best time to visit Cusco

### High, shoulder, and low season

The **high season** (May to September) concentrates the best weather: sunny days, clear skies, and cold nights. July and August are the peak months, with hotels at 90 % occupancy and high prices. It is the best time for trekking and visiting Machu Picchu.

The **shoulder season** (April and October-November) still offers good weather with fewer tourists. September is particularly recommended for its combination of stable weather and lower crowds.

The **low season** (December to March) is the rainy season. Sunny days alternate with afternoon showers. The advantages: cheaper hotels, fewer tourists, and greener landscapes. January and February are the rainiest months; the Inca Trail closes all of February for maintenance.

### Monthly weather and packing recommendations

Cusco has a dry and temperate climate most of the year. Daytime temperatures range between 64-72 °F (18-22 °C) year-round. Nights are cold, especially from May to August, when they can drop to 32 °F (0 °C).

Always wear layers: a thermal shirt, fleece or vest, waterproof jacket, and windbreaker. Closed hiking shoes, preferably waterproof. High-factor sunscreen (the sun burns faster at altitude) and UV-protection sunglasses.

## What to see and do in Cusco

### City must-sees

The **Plaza de Armas** with the Cathedral and the Church of the Society of Jesus. **Qoricancha** (Inca Sun Temple) with the Santo Domingo convent built over it. The ruins of **Sacsayhuamán**, the ceremonial fortress of cyclopean stones. The **San Blas** neighborhood, with artisan workshops and cobblestone streets. **San Pedro Market**, for juices and local foods. The **Center for Traditional Textiles** to learn about Andean weaving techniques.

### Nearby excursions

The **Sacred Valley of the Incas** (Pisac, Ollantaytambo, Moray, Maras, and Chinchero) can be visited in a full day. **Rainbow Mountain (Vinicunca)** at 17,060 ft (5,200 m), accessible from Cusco on a full-day tour (requires good physical condition). **Humantay Lagoon**, a turquoise lake at the foot of the Humantay glacier. The **Lares hot springs** and the alternative Inca Trail route. The **Choquequirao stone forest**, a less crowded alternative to Machu Picchu.

## How many days you need in Cusco

### Quick stop (2-3 days)

Day 1: arrival, acclimatization, and morning city tour (Plaza de Armas, Qoricancha, Sacsayhuamán). Day 2: express Sacred Valley (Pisac, Ollantaytambo, return to Cusco). Day 3: Machu Picchu full day (train to Aguas Calientes, guided visit, return).

### Classic trip (4-5 days)

Days 1-2: acclimatization in Cusco with city tour and market visits. Day 3: full Sacred Valley (Pisac, Ollantaytambo, Moray, Maras). Day 4: Machu Picchu full day. Day 5: free day for Rainbow Mountain, Humantay, or shopping in San Blas.

### Extended itinerary (6-8 days)

Add the classic Inca Trail (4 days/3 nights) or the Salkantay route (5 days). Include Rainbow Mountain or Humantay Lagoon excursion. Combine with a bike ride to the Maras Salt Mines. Add days in the South Valley (Tipón, Piquillacta, Andahuaylillas).

## Estimated budget for traveling to Cusco

### Sample budget per person

**Budget (25-40 USD/day)**: shared hostels, market lunches, group tours, entrance to archaeological sites with tourist ticket.

**Standard (60-100 USD/day)**: 3-star hotel with breakfast included, mid-range restaurants, group tours with English/Spanish guide, Expedition train to Machu Picchu.

**Premium (from 150 USD/day)**: boutique or 5-star hotel, private tours with guide, Vistadome or Hiram Bingham train, dinners at high-end Andean cuisine restaurants.

## Altitude, safety, and health tips

### Altitude sickness: symptoms and how to prevent it

Cusco is at 11,155 ft (3,400 m). Altitude sickness (soroche) manifests with headache, fatigue, nausea, insomnia, and loss of appetite. To prevent it: arrive the first day without strenuous activity, drink coca tea, hydrate with 2-3 liters of water daily, avoid heavy meals and alcohol. Natural acclimatization takes 24-48 hours. Pharmacies sell altitude sickness pills (Acetazolamide) without prescription, and medical oxygen is available at hotels.

### Safe areas and common scams

Cusco's historic center is safe to walk day and night, with a police presence on main streets. The most common scams are: unauthorized taxis (use ride-hailing apps like Uber or Indrive), street currency exchange (use banks or authorized exchange houses), unlicensed street tours (only hire agencies registered with MINCETUR), and people inviting you to "ceremonies" or "readings" with undisclosed costs.

## Best tours and experiences in Cusco

Cusco is the ideal base for Peru's most iconic tours. The **Machu Picchu full day** is the most requested: train + bus + guide combo from Cusco. The **full Sacred Valley** tour covers Pisac, Ollantaytambo, Moray, and Maras in one day. For adventurers, the **4-day Inca Trail** is South America's most famous trekking experience, arriving at Machu Picchu through the Sun Gate. There are also experiential tourism options in Quechua communities, Andean cooking classes, and visits to traditional textile centers.
