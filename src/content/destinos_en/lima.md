## Quick summary: why visit Lima

Lima is the entry point for 90 % of travelers arriving in Peru, but it is not just a connection hub. It is the gastronomic capital of Latin America, with restaurants among the world's top 50. Its historic center is a UNESCO World Heritage site, and its modern districts offer a unique mix of culture, art, and coastal living.

It is the ideal destination for food travelers, art and history lovers, surfers, and those seeking a cosmopolitan city with direct access to the coast, the Ica vineyards, and nearby archaeological sites.

## When is the best time to visit Lima

### High, shoulder, and low season

The **high season** (December to April) coincides with the austral summer: sunny days, temperatures of 78-82 °F (26-28 °C), and clear skies. January and February are the warmest months and ideal for beach and surf. It is also a season of celebrations and festivals.

The **shoulder season** (May and November) offers temperate weather with fewer tourists. It is a good time for city tours and food experiences without crowds.

The **low season** (June to October) is characterized by overcast skies and drizzle (garúa). Temperatures drop to 59-68 °F (15-20 °C). It is the best time to find cheaper flights and hotels, and to enjoy Lima's cuisine without lines.

### Monthly weather and packing recommendations

Lima has a subtropical desert climate. In summer (January-March) the weather is warm and humid: pack light cotton clothing, sunscreen, sunglasses, and a light windbreaker. In winter (June-September) the weather is overcast and cool: pack a jacket, scarf, and closed shoes. Always have an umbrella or light rain jacket on hand.

## What to see and do in Lima

### City must-sees

The **Historic Center** (Plaza Mayor, Cathedral, Government Palace, San Francisco Catacombs). **Miraflores** with the **coastal cliff walk (Malecón)**, **Love Park**, and Larcomar shopping center. **Barranco**, the bohemian district with murals, art galleries, and the Bridge of Sighs. The **Larco Museum** with the best collection of pre-Columbian erotic pottery. **Huaca Pucllana**, a ceremonial pyramid in the middle of Miraflores.

### Nearby excursions

**Paracas and the Ballestas Islands** (3.5 hours away), the "poor man's Galapagos" with Humboldt penguins, sea lions, and guano birds. **Huacachina and the Ica dunes** (4 hours away), for sandboarding and dune buggies. The **Nazca Lines** by scenic flight from Pisco or Nazca. The **Ica and Lunahuaná vineyards** for wine tourism. **Pachacámac**, the most important coastal archaeological sanctuary in Peru.

## How many days you need in Lima

### Quick stop (1-2 days)

Day 1: Historic Center in the morning and Miraflores/Barranco in the afternoon. Day 2: food tour (Surquillo Market, cooking classes) or museum visit.

### Classic trip (3 days)

Days 1-2: full city tour (Center, Miraflores, Barranco). Day 2 can also include Huaca Pucllana and Larco Museum. Day 3: full-day excursion to Paracas or food tour with dinner at a high-end restaurant.

### Extended itinerary (4-5 days)

Add an excursion to Huacachina and Ica (2 days/1 night) for sandboarding and vineyard visits. Include surfing at Costa Verde or Peruvian cooking classes. Explore Pachacámac and the Asia beach resort.

## Estimated budget for traveling to Lima

### Sample budget per person per day

**Budget (25-40 USD/day)**: hostels in the Historic Center, market menus, public transport, basic museums.

**Standard (60-100 USD/day)**: 3-star hotel in Miraflores, mid-range restaurants, Uber, guided city tour.

**Premium (from 180 USD/day)**: 5-star hotel in San Isidro or Miraflores, high-end restaurants, private tours, dinner at a world-class restaurant.

## Safety and health tips

### Safe areas and how to get around

Miraflores, Barranco, and San Isidro are safe districts to walk day and night. The Historic Center is safe during the day with caution; at night, use a taxi or Uber. Always use ride-hailing apps (Uber, Cabify, Didi) or official taxis from hotels and restaurants. Avoid unmarked street taxis. Minibuses (combis) are not recommended for tourists for safety and comfort reasons.

### Health and practical tips

Lima is at sea level, no risk of altitude sickness. Tap water is not potable for tourists; use bottled water. Street food is safe at stalls with high turnover. Raw ceviche is safe at established restaurants. Wear sunscreen even on cloudy days (UV radiation penetrates clouds).

## Best tours and experiences in Lima

**Food city tours** are Lima's star experience: tours of traditional markets, Peruvian cooking classes, and dinners at restaurants like Central, Maido, or Astrid & Gastón. **Street art tours in Barranco** cover the murals and galleries of the bohemian district. **Excursions to Paracas and Huacachina** combine nature, desert, and sea in a weekend.
