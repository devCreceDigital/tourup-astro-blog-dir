## Quick summary: why travel to Puno

Puno is the folkloric capital of Peru and the gateway to Lake Titicaca, the highest navigable lake in the world (12,556 ft / 3,827 m). Here, Quechua and Aymara cultures meet on floating totora reed islands, with millennia-old textiles and festivals like the Candelaria Feast, one of the largest in Latin America.

It is the ideal destination for travelers interested in living cultures, community experiences, and high mountain landscapes. It is the piece that connects southern Peru with Bolivia on a classic itinerary.

## When is the best time to visit Puno

### High, shoulder, and low season

The **high season** (May to October) offers dry weather, clear skies, and calm lake conditions, ideal for navigation and island visits. The Candelaria Feast (February) attracts thousands of visitors even though it is the rainy season.

The **shoulder season** (April and October) combines good weather with fewer tourists.

The **low season** (November to March) has frequent rain and overcast skies. Advantages include fewer tourists, lower prices, and greater accommodation availability.

### Monthly weather and packing recommendations

Puno has a cold and dry climate most of the year. Daytime temperatures reach 59-64 °F (15-18 °C), but nights drop to 32 °F (0 °C) or less from May to August. Pack thermal clothing, a hooded jacket, gloves, scarf, hat, and waterproof shoes. Sunscreen and sunglasses are essential due to high-altitude radiation.

## What to see and do in Puno

### Must-sees in the city and lake

**Lake Titicaca** and its islands. The **Uros Floating Islands**, built entirely of totora reed, inhabited by the Uro community. **Taquile Island**, with textiles declared UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage. **Amantaní Island**, where you can sleep with local families and climb to the Pachamama temple. **Soto Island** and **Suasi Island**, less visited but equally beautiful.

### In the city of Puno

The **Plaza de Armas** with the Andean baroque Cathedral. **Kuntur Wasi viewpoint** with panoramic views of the city and lake. The **Porteño neighborhood** and the tourist waterfront. **Dreyer Museum** with pre-Columbian pieces from the Tiahuanaco culture. The **Candelaria Feast** in February, declared UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage, with dances, music, and traditional costumes.

## How many days you need in Puno

### Quick stop (2 days)

Day 1: arrival, afternoon in the city (Plaza de Armas, viewpoint, waterfront). Day 2: lake tour (Uros + Taquile) by shared boat, return in the afternoon.

### Classic trip (3-4 days)

Days 1-2: same as quick stop plus overnight on Amantaní with a local family. Day 3: return from Amantaní with Taquile visit. Day 4: Sillustani (funerary chullpas) and departure to Arequipa or Cusco.

### Extended itinerary (5-7 days)

Add the route to Bolivia (Copacabana, Island of the Sun, La Paz). Include Sillustani and other pre-Inca chullpas. Combine with a trekking on the Capachica peninsula.

## Altitude, safety, and health tips

### Altitude sickness in Puno

Puno is at 12,556 ft (3,827 m), higher than Cusco. If you come directly from Lima or Arequipa (sea level), altitude sickness symptoms will be stronger. The ideal is to arrive via Cusco (prior acclimatization). Traditional remedies (coca tea, coca leaves) work. Medical oxygen is available at hotels. On the first day, avoid strenuous activity and take it easy.

### Safety

Puno is safe in tourist areas. The center and waterfront have good daytime traffic. At night, use ride-hailing apps. On the lake, tours are safe and boats have life jackets. The island communities have been receiving tourists for decades and are accustomed to international visitors.

## Best tours and experiences in Puno

The **Uros and Taquile full day** is the classic experience: navigation on Lake Titicaca, visit to the totora reed floating islands, and a hike to Taquile's main square. **Overnight on Amantaní** includes accommodation with a local family, a typical dinner, and ceremonies. The **Sillustani tour** visits the funerary tombs of the Colla culture on the shores of Lake Umayo. For the more adventurous, the **Titicaca cruise to Bolivia** is a multi-day navigation experience.
